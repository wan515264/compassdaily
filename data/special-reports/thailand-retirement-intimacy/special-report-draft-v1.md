# Special Report Draft v1｜特别报道初稿

## Working Title｜工作标题

**当养老金遇见衰老：泰国跨国婚姻中的权力与照护**

**When Pensions Meet Ageing: Power and Care in Thai–Western Marriages**

## Research Question｜研究问题

**How does power shift when Western husbands grow old in Thailand?**  
**当西方丈夫在泰国老去，跨国婚姻中的权力如何重新配置？**

## Central Argument｜核心论点

跨国婚姻中的权力并不是一件可以由一方完整转交给另一方的物品。对部分迁居泰国的西方丈夫而言，养老金、国籍、资产与全球收入差距带来的优势，可能在晚年继续存在；与此同时，疾病、语言障碍、医疗制度和日常生活又会增加他们对泰国妻子的身体照护、地方知识与亲属网络的依赖。这里发生的不是简单的 **power transfer**，而是 **power reconfiguration**：经济、法律、身体、语言、家庭和退出能力等不同维度的权力，在关系的生命历程中被重新分配和协商。

Power in transnational marriage is not something that passes intact from one partner to the other. For some Western husbands who settle in Thailand, the advantages conferred by pensions, nationality, assets, and global income inequality may persist into later life. At the same time, illness, language barriers, health systems, and everyday practicalities may make them increasingly dependent on their Thai wives for bodily care, local knowledge, and kinship networks. What occurs is not a simple **power transfer**, but a **power reconfiguration**: economic, legal, bodily, linguistic, familial, and exit-related forms of power are redistributed and renegotiated across the life course of a relationship.

## Evidence Limitations｜证据局限

本文不讨论所有西方退休者，也不把退休迁移等同于跨国婚姻或性旅游。现有研究以小规模质性研究为主，地点、国籍和关系轨迹差异很大。关于临终照护最直接的论文只深入记录了 **3名泰国女性**；关于泰国东北部外籍男性医疗处境的研究虽然访问了42名利益相关者，其中男性外籍居民只有 **9人**。两项研究只能帮助辨认机制，不能估算普遍程度。全国人口研究发表于2023年，但使用的是2010年人口普查资料，不能代表2026年的实时规模。丧偶、继承、养老金延续、住房安全、离婚和关系退出仍缺少系统证据。

This report does not speak for all Western retirees, nor does it equate retirement migration with transnational marriage or sex tourism. Most of the available research consists of small qualitative studies conducted in different places, among different nationalities, and across different relationship trajectories. The most direct study of end-of-life care examines only **three Thai women** in depth. A study of foreign men’s healthcare experiences in Northeast Thailand interviewed 42 stakeholders, but only **nine were male expatriate residents**. These studies can help identify mechanisms; they cannot estimate prevalence. A national demographic study was published in 2023 but relied on the 2010 census, so it cannot represent the situation in 2026. Systematic evidence remains scarce on widowhood, inheritance, pension continuation, housing security, divorce, and relationship exit.

## 一｜退休生活的许诺

### The Promise of Retirement

2026年，澳大利亚广播公司报道了越来越多退休者进行的一笔计算：如果养老金在本国越来越难以覆盖房租、保险和基本生活，把它带到泰国，是否能换来更宽裕的晚年？一位迁居泰国的澳大利亚养老金领取者说，在澳大利亚地区城市难以负担的生活，在泰国变得可行。但报道也提醒，日常消费较低并不等于总体风险较低；汇率、签证、医疗保险、家庭距离和突发事件，随时可能改写这笔账。[N11]

In 2026, the Australian Broadcasting Corporation reported on a calculation being made by a growing number of retirees: if a pension can no longer comfortably cover rent, insurance, and basic living costs at home, could taking it to Thailand provide a more secure old age? An Australian pensioner who had moved to Thailand said that a life he could not afford in regional Australia had become possible there. Yet the report also warned that lower everyday costs do not necessarily mean lower overall risk. Exchange rates, visas, health insurance, distance from family, and unexpected events can alter the calculation at any time.[N11]

这首先是一个 **retirement migration（退休迁移）** 的故事，而不是一个“去泰国找妻子”的故事。清迈的退休社区、医疗旅游基础、长住签证和照护市场，让泰国成为东南亚养老产业的一部分。Channel NewsAsia在2026年的调查中记录，一些面向外国人的清迈养老机构提供语言、饮食与全天候照护服务；当时报道所列的部分房费从每月25,000泰铢起，相关退休签证通常要求申请人满足存款或月收入条件。[N10] 泰国旅游业界也公开主张以健康、疗养和长期停留产品吸引外国退休者。[N14] 目的地并非只由个人偏好自然形成，也由政策、商业和“可购买的舒适生活”共同制造。

This is first a story of **retirement migration**, not a story about “going to Thailand to find a wife.” Chiang Mai’s retirement communities, medical-tourism infrastructure, long-stay visas, and care market have made Thailand part of Southeast Asia’s retirement industry. A 2026 Channel NewsAsia investigation found that some Chiang Mai facilities serving foreign residents offered language support, tailored meals, and round-the-clock care. At the time, some rooms cited in the report started at 25,000 baht per month, while relevant retirement visas generally required applicants to meet savings or monthly-income thresholds.[N10] Thailand’s tourism industry has also publicly advocated attracting foreign retirees through health, wellness, and long-stay products.[N14] Retirement destinations are not produced by personal preference alone; they are also shaped by policy, commerce, and the promise of purchasable comfort.

这种吸引力建立在一组跨国差价之上：养老金的购买力、原居国与泰国的生活成本差、服务劳动价格，以及护照和福利制度所提供的流动能力。Sarah Scuzzarello对20名60岁以上白人西方退休移民的访谈显示，一些受访者在泰国重新获得了在原居国因年龄和阶级位置而减弱的地位感。男性受访者尤其可能把提供者身份、年轻伴侣和“第二次青春”连在一起。她把这种“good life”解释为一种被实践出来的特权：它并不只来自个人努力，也依赖全球收入与种族等级。[L04]

This appeal rests on a set of cross-border differentials: pension purchasing power, disparities in living costs between countries of origin and Thailand, the price of service labour, and the mobility enabled by passports and welfare systems. Sarah Scuzzarello’s interviews with 20 white Western retirement migrants aged over 60 found that some participants regained in Thailand a sense of status diminished by age and class position at home. Male participants in particular sometimes linked the provider role, a younger partner, and a “second youth.” Scuzzarello interprets this “good life” as practised privilege—one produced not only by individual effort, but also by global income and racial hierarchies.[L04]

但“西方退休者”并不是一个内部同质的富裕群体。Megan Lafferty与Kristen Maher在泰国东北部的民族志研究指出，一些西方男性拥有相对泰国当地收入更强的购买力，却同时面对固定收入缩水、签证压力、健康恶化、财产不稳和关系依赖。[U03] 同一笔养老金可以在两个国家呈现完全不同的阶级意义；相对优势也可能在医疗账单、保险缺口和长期照护面前迅速变薄。

Yet “Western retirees” are not a homogeneous wealthy group. Megan Lafferty and Kristen Maher’s ethnographic research in Northeast Thailand shows that some Western men possess greater purchasing power relative to local incomes while also confronting shrinking fixed incomes, visa pressure, deteriorating health, insecure property arrangements, and relationship dependence.[U03] The same pension can carry radically different class meanings in two countries, and relative advantage can quickly narrow under medical bills, insurance gaps, and long-term care costs.

因此，退休幻想的第一层矛盾已经出现：一个人在全球结构中占据优势，并不意味着他在晚年生活的每一个维度都安全。可负担的阳光、住房与服务，既可能是真实改善，也可能把未来风险推迟到身体衰弱之后。

The first contradiction of the retirement fantasy is therefore already visible: occupying an advantaged position in the global order does not guarantee security in every dimension of later life. Affordable sunshine, housing, and services may represent a real improvement, but they may also postpone risk until the body begins to fail.

还需要保留来源国的差异。本文材料中的“Western”涵盖欧洲、北美和澳大利亚，它只是研究沿用的宽泛分类，并不意味着各国养老金、医保、家庭制度和个人阶级位置相同。正因为这些制度条件不同，同样是“在泰国退休”，可以对应相当不同的资源与风险。

Differences among countries of origin must also remain visible. In the sources used here, “Western” encompasses Europe, North America, and Australia. It is a broad category inherited from the literature, not a claim that pension, healthcare, family systems, and individual class positions are identical across these places. Because these institutional conditions differ, “retiring in Thailand” may involve very different resources and risks.

## 二｜爱、金钱与家庭

### Love, Money, and Family

退休迁移并不自动产生跨国婚姻，但旅游、工作、婚姻迁移与退休生活确实可能在一段生命史中交叉。Paul Statham、Sarah Scuzzarello、Sirijit Sunanta与Alexander Trupp把泰国与西方之间的流动概括为高度性别化的“both-ways migration”：泰国女性可能先因婚姻迁往欧洲、北美或澳大利亚，丈夫退休后，夫妻又迁回泰国，或者在两地之间季节性往返。[U01] 这不是所有夫妻必经的路线，却说明“妻子向西迁移”和“丈夫向泰退休”可能是同一段关系的不同阶段。

Retirement migration does not automatically produce transnational marriage, but tourism, work, marriage migration, and retirement can intersect within a single life history. Paul Statham, Sarah Scuzzarello, Sirijit Sunanta, and Alexander Trupp describe movement between Thailand and “the West” as highly gendered “both-ways migration”: a Thai woman may first move to Europe, North America, or Australia through marriage; after her husband retires, the couple may move to Thailand or travel seasonally between the two places.[U01] This is not a route followed by every couple, but it shows how a wife’s westward migration and a husband’s retirement in Thailand may form different stages of the same relationship.

关系如何开始，不能被压缩成“爱情”或“金钱”中的一个答案。Sirijit Sunanta在泰国东北部两个村庄进行的六个月民族志显示，跨国婚姻与当地女性此前到城市工厂、建筑业和服务业工作的迁移历史相连。汇款会进入父母生活、子女教育、住房、寺庙和学校；成为“global wife”并没有取消女性作为“local daughter”的责任。女性可以借婚姻移动、组织资源并改变家庭位置，但她们的选择仍受到区域贫困、劳动机会和“好女儿”义务塑造。[L02]

How a relationship begins cannot be reduced to a choice between “love” and “money.” Sirijit Sunanta’s six-month ethnography in two villages in Northeast Thailand connects transnational marriage with women’s earlier migration to urban factories, construction, and service work. Remittances flow into parents’ livelihoods, children’s education, housing, temples, and schools; becoming a “global wife” does not cancel a woman’s responsibilities as a “local daughter.” Women can use marriage to move, organise resources, and alter their position within the family, but their choices remain shaped by regional poverty, employment opportunities, and the obligations of the “dutiful daughter.”[L02]

The Isaan Record记录过一位女性的叙述：她最初需要偿债并抚养孩子，关系后来才发展出爱情。报道据此拒绝把感情与经济支持视为互相污染的两种动机。[N03] 另一篇报道走进乌隆他尼一个跨国夫妻较集中的社区：退休挪威男性Rolf依靠约每月70,000至80,000泰铢的养老金生活；资金不仅进入夫妻住房，也通过商店、学校和寺庙进入地方社会。报道中的具体金额和村庄估计只是个案与地方说法，不能推广为泰国全国模式；它的价值在于显示，养老金一旦进入婚姻，就不再只是丈夫个人的退休收入，而可能成为跨国家庭与地方网络中的资源。[N02]

The Isaan Record has documented one woman’s account of entering a relationship initially because she needed to repay debt and support her children; love developed later. The report therefore refuses to treat affection and economic support as mutually contaminating motives.[N03] Another report visited an Udon Thani community with a substantial concentration of transnational couples. Rolf, a retired Norwegian man, lived on a pension of roughly 70,000 to 80,000 baht per month. The money supported not only the couple’s home but also circulated through shops, schools, and temples. These figures and village estimates are case-specific local accounts and cannot be generalised across Thailand. Their value lies in showing that once a pension enters a marriage, it may cease to be merely the husband’s retirement income and become a resource within a transnational family and local network.[N02]

与此同时，资源流动不等于女性只是接受者。她们可能选择伴侣、管理汇款、照顾子女、组织返迁、经营生意、争取房屋或正式婚姻权利，也可能拒绝某些要求。**Agency（能动性）** 与结构不平等并不互相排斥：一个选择可以具有策略性，同时仍由有限的工作机会、家庭债务、国籍制度和性别责任所塑造。

At the same time, the movement of resources does not make women passive recipients. They may choose partners, manage remittances, care for children, organise return migration, run businesses, pursue housing or formal marital rights, and refuse particular demands. **Agency** and structural inequality are not mutually exclusive: a choice can be strategic while still being shaped by limited employment opportunities, family debt, nationality regimes, and gendered responsibilities.

这种复杂性也能在长期关系中看到。Statham对20名与欧洲、澳大利亚或北美男性维持7至30年关系的泰国女性进行传记式访谈。他发现，最初的物质、性或情感交换会随着结婚、迁移、共同生活、资产安排和衰老而改变。正式婚姻以及可执行的福利、财产和继承权，可能提高女性的长期安全；感情与信任可能增长，也可能伴随控制、孤立和经济不稳。[L03] 不过，这20人全部来自至少维持七年的关系，早期破裂者已被研究设计排除，所以论文不能告诉我们成功与失败各占多少。

This complexity is also visible in long-term relationships. Statham conducted biographical interviews with 20 Thai women whose relationships with European, Australian, or North American men had lasted between seven and thirty years. He found that initial material, sexual, or emotional exchanges changed through marriage, migration, cohabitation, asset arrangements, and ageing. Formal marriage and enforceable welfare, property, and inheritance rights could improve women’s long-term security; affection and trust could grow, but control, isolation, and financial insecurity could also persist.[L03] All 20 participants, however, came from relationships lasting at least seven years. Early relationship breakdowns were excluded by design, so the study cannot tell us how frequently relationships succeed or fail.

更准确的表达不是“爱战胜了金钱”，也不是“爱情只是交易的伪装”，而是：**love + money + obligation + care + strategy** 可以同时存在，并且会随时间改变意义。爱与钱无法被干净分开，正因为亲密关系本来就发生在住房、签证、家庭责任、疾病和未来计划之中。

The more accurate formulation is neither “love overcame money” nor “love was only a disguise for transaction.” It is that **love + money + obligation + care + strategy** can coexist and change meaning over time. Love and money cannot be cleanly separated because intimacy unfolds within housing, visas, family responsibilities, illness, and plans for the future.

纪录片《Heartbound》让这种时间性变得可见。影片用十年追踪多组泰国女性与丹麦男性的关系：其中既有从芭堤雅性工作和跨国媒合网络开始的婚姻，也有迁往丹麦后的工厂劳动、持续汇款、关系失败、返乡和照顾患病亲人的经历。[N05] 这些人物不能代表整个泰西婚姻群体，丹麦经验也不能直接替代在泰退休夫妻；但长时间跟拍纠正了新闻常见的“相遇瞬间”。年龄差与收入差在第一张照片中最醒目，随后出现的却是轮班工作、子女、娘家、语言、孤独和共同老去。

The documentary *Heartbound* makes this temporality visible. Filmed over ten years, it follows relationships between several Thai women and Danish men. These include marriages emerging from sex work in Pattaya and transnational matchmaking networks, as well as factory labour after migration to Denmark, continuing remittances, relationship breakdown, return, and care for an ill relative.[N05] The people in the film cannot represent all Thai–Western marriages, and the Danish experience cannot stand in for retired couples in Thailand. Yet its long timeframe corrects journalism’s habitual focus on the moment of encounter. Age and income gaps dominate the first image; what follows includes shift work, children, natal families, language, loneliness, and growing old together.

它也提示，媒体使用什么时间尺度，会改变我们以为自己看见的关系。如果镜头只停在旅游区，退休移民容易被等同于性旅游；如果只拍婚礼，经济结构会被浪漫叙事遮住；如果只在丈夫患病时进入家庭，妻子多年以前的迁移和劳动又会消失。把关系放回生命历程，不是为了替任何一方作道德辩护，而是为了看见资源与责任如何累积，以及同一种选择为什么可能同时带来机会、亲密和约束。

It also shows how the timeframe chosen by the media changes the relationship we think we see. If the camera remains in tourist districts, retirement migration can become indistinguishable from sex tourism. If it records only the wedding, romantic language may obscure economic structures. If it enters the home only when a husband becomes ill, the wife’s earlier years of migration and labour disappear. Restoring the life course is not a moral defence of either partner. It allows us to see how resources and responsibilities accumulate—and why the same choice can produce opportunity, intimacy, and constraint at once.

## 三｜回到泰国，开始老去

### Going Home, Growing Old

“回泰国养老”听起来像一个完成时：夫妻选定地点，开始退休生活。但Panitee Brown对12对从荷兰移居泰国、并已在泰生活3至12年的泰荷夫妻研究显示，返迁更像一条反复调整的轨迹。夫妻可能经历等待、试住、季节往返、再次迁移和未来照护规划。护照、语言、养老金、房产、工作经验和亲属网络构成不同的 **mobility capital（流动资本）**；它们并不平均掌握在夫妻双方手中。[U08]

“Returning to Thailand to retire” sounds like a completed action: a couple selects a place and begins retirement. Panitee Brown’s study of 12 Thai–Dutch couples who had moved from the Netherlands and lived in Thailand for three to twelve years, however, shows return as a repeatedly adjusted trajectory. Couples may wait, test a move, travel seasonally, migrate again, and plan for future care. Passports, language, pensions, property, employment experience, and kinship networks constitute different forms of **mobility capital**, and they are not distributed evenly between partners.[U08]

在欧洲，泰国妻子的学历、职业经验和社会关系可能贬值；回到泰国后，她的语言、地方知识与亲属网络则更有价值。Manasigan Kanchanachitra与Pattraporn Chuenglertsiri分别访问13名居泰欧洲丈夫和13名从欧洲返泰的泰国妻子，发现迁移目的地会改变双方的社会位置：欧洲男性在泰国经常经历地位上升，泰国女性在欧洲却可能面对偏见、孤立和安全网不足；男性的优势又会随健康、时间和对妻子的依赖而减弱。[U02] 两组受访者并不是同一批夫妻，这项研究不能重建具体婚姻的变化，却清楚说明权力不是个人随身携带、到哪里都同样有效的属性。

In Europe, a Thai wife’s qualifications, professional experience, and social ties may lose value; after returning to Thailand, her language, local knowledge, and kinship networks may become more valuable. Manasigan Kanchanachitra and Pattraporn Chuenglertsiri interviewed 13 European husbands living in Thailand and 13 Thai wives who had returned from Europe. They found that destination reshapes social position: European men often experienced upward status mobility in Thailand, while Thai women in Europe could encounter prejudice, isolation, and weak social and emotional safety nets. Men’s advantages could also diminish with time, poorer health, and growing dependence on their wives.[U02] The two groups were not married to one another, so the study cannot reconstruct change within particular marriages. It does, however, show that power is not a personal possession that works equally everywhere.

身体老化使这种地点差异变得更具体。2021年，一项关于泰国东北部“西方女婿”医疗处境的质性研究通过深度访谈和焦点小组访问42名参与者，包括医疗、移民、政策和学术等利益相关者；其中男性外籍居民只有9名。研究识别出慢性病、保险不足、私立医院费用、语言障碍和对公立医疗的不信任等问题。[U06] 这些发现不能被写成“多数西方退休者没有保险”或某种患病率，因为直接受访男性太少；它能提示的是，在泰国拥有消费能力，并不等于能够独立理解和进入医疗系统。

Ageing bodies make these place-based differences more concrete. In 2021, a qualitative study of healthcare among “Western sons-in-law” in Northeast Thailand used interviews and focus groups with 42 participants, including stakeholders in healthcare, immigration, policy, and academia. Only nine participants were male expatriate residents. The study identified chronic illness, insufficient insurance, private-hospital costs, language barriers, and distrust of public hospitals as relevant problems.[U06] Because so few expatriate men were directly interviewed, these findings cannot be rewritten as “most Western retirees are uninsured” or used to estimate disease prevalence. What they suggest is that purchasing power in Thailand does not necessarily confer the ability to understand or independently navigate its healthcare system.

此时，婚姻中的地方能力开始显现。谁能读懂泰文文件？谁知道该去哪个医院、如何和医护沟通、向哪些亲属求助？谁能在夜间陪护、处理药物、安排交通？丈夫仍可能拥有养老金、护照和跨国资产，妻子却可能掌握让生活在泰国实际运转的语言、关系和照护知识。双方的资源没有合并成一个总分；不同资源在不同场景中生效。

At this point, local capacities within the marriage come into view. Who can read Thai documents? Who knows which hospital to use, how to communicate with medical staff, or which relatives to call? Who can provide night-time care, manage medication, and arrange transport? A husband may still possess a pension, passport, and transnational assets, while his wife may command the language, relationships, and care knowledge that make life in Thailand function. Their resources do not combine into a single score; different resources become effective in different situations.

这也解释了为什么不能把“回到妻子的家乡”自动写成女性获胜。Statham关于“unintended transnationalism”的研究使用与其2020年论文相同的20名长期关系女性资料，发现一些女性即使回到泰国，仍可能因长期迁移、与子女分离或丈夫坚持西式生活而感到与家庭和社会疏离。[U05] 地理上的回家，不必然带来文化归属、经济自主或退出自由。

This also explains why “returning to the wife’s home” cannot automatically be written as a female victory. Statham’s study of “unintended transnationalism,” based on the same 20 women in long-term relationships as his 2020 article, found that some women remained alienated from family and Thai society even after returning, as a result of long migration, separation from children, or a husband’s insistence on Western ways of living.[U05] A geographical homecoming does not necessarily produce cultural belonging, economic autonomy, or the freedom to leave.

## 四｜当特权遇见依赖

### When Privilege Meets Dependency

如果把权力只理解为“谁付钱”，丈夫的优势似乎很清楚；如果只看医院、村庄或家中的日常运转，妻子的作用又可能更突出。但这两种观察并不互相推翻。

If power is understood only as “who pays,” the husband’s advantage may appear obvious. If we look only at how a hospital visit, village life, or a household operates, the wife’s role may appear more decisive. These two observations do not cancel one another.

在关系早期，全球收入差、白人身份、男性位置和婚姻移民制度可能让丈夫拥有更大的选择权。他可能决定在哪里生活、如何分配养老金、是否正式结婚，以及哪些资产对妻子具有可执行的权利。部分研究也记录了“年轻、顺从、家庭导向、善于照顾人的亚洲妻子”这种 **racialised desire（种族化欲望）**。它在一些男性叙述中真实存在，却不能代表所有西方男性，更不能被当作泰国女性的真实本质。[L03][L04]

Earlier in a relationship, global income disparities, whiteness, male social position, and marriage-migration regimes may grant a husband greater choice. He may decide where the couple lives, how a pension is distributed, whether they formally marry, and which assets provide his wife with enforceable rights. Some studies also document a form of **racialised desire** centred on the imagined “young, submissive, family-oriented Asian wife who knows how to care for a man.” It appears in some men’s accounts, but it cannot represent all Western men—and it must never be treated as an authentic essence of Thai women.[L03][L04]

随着丈夫老去，同一个人可能在另一组关系中变得依赖。他可能需要妻子翻译、协调医疗、联系家属、管理日常开支，甚至帮助洗浴、进食和移动。这里可以使用“**economic privilege × embodied dependency（经济特权 × 身体性依赖）**”作为观察框架：它并不是已经被单一研究验证的正式理论，也不是一条“权力必然反转”的定律，而是帮助我们辨认两种状态如何同时存在。

As a husband grows old, the same person may become dependent within another set of relations. He may need his wife to translate, coordinate healthcare, contact relatives, manage daily spending, or help him bathe, eat, and move. **Economic privilege × embodied dependency** can be used here as an interpretive frame. It is neither a formal theory validated by a single study nor a law according to which power inevitably reverses. It helps us recognise how two conditions may coexist.

妻子在日常决策中的影响力可能增加，但丈夫的国籍、养老金与资产优势并不会因此消失。反过来，丈夫的身体脆弱也不会取消他可能拥有的性别和经济控制。权力因此被拆成多个维度：经济资源、法律身份、房产、语言、地方网络、身体自主、照护能力，以及一段关系中真正可以离开的能力。它们可能由不同的人掌握，也可能在泰国与欧洲、健康与疾病、婚前与婚后呈现不同组合。

A wife’s influence over everyday decisions may increase, but her husband’s advantages in nationality, pension income, and assets do not thereby disappear. Conversely, his bodily vulnerability does not erase whatever gendered or economic control he may retain. Power therefore separates into multiple dimensions: economic resources, legal status, property, language, local networks, bodily autonomy, caregiving capacity, and the actual ability to leave a relationship. Different partners may control different dimensions, and the combination may change between Thailand and Europe, health and illness, or before and after marriage.

全国人口资料还提醒我们，不要把最醒目的媒体图像当成全部现实。2023年发表的一项研究使用泰国2010年人口普查1%微观样本，估算当时约有60,220对同户居住、可识别的泰西异性夫妻；约三分之一西方配偶在60岁以上，多数夫妻居住在城市，东北地区约占15%。[U09] 这组数据已陈旧，也遗漏未登记、分居、同性与跨国家庭，不能描述2020年代现状；但它足以校正“所有关系都是伊善年轻农村女性与富裕老年男人”的单一想象。

National demographic data also warn against treating the most familiar media image as the whole reality. A study published in 2023 used a one-percent micro-sample of Thailand’s 2010 census to estimate that roughly 60,220 identifiable Thai–Western heterosexual married couples were living in the same household at that time. Around one-third of the Western partners were aged 60 or above; most couples lived in urban areas, and the Northeast accounted for about 15 percent.[U09] The data are old and exclude unregistered, separated, same-sex, and transnational households, so they cannot describe the 2020s. They can, however, correct the singular image in which every relationship consists of a young rural Isan woman and a wealthy older man.

所以，丈夫老去时发生的不是 **power transfer**，而是 **power reconfiguration**。问题不再只是“谁更有权力”，而是：哪一种权力，在什么地点、什么身体状态和什么制度关系中，能够真正转化为安全与选择？

What happens as a husband ages is therefore not **power transfer**, but **power reconfiguration**. The question is no longer simply who has more power, but which form of power—under which geographical, bodily, and institutional conditions—can actually be converted into security and choice.

## 五｜照护的代价

### The Cost of Care

Patcharin Lapanun等人在2021至2022年间于乌隆他尼和孔敬开展多点民族志，访问和观察了32名参与者，其中包括13对泰西夫妻、5名丈夫未在场的泰国妻子，以及1名已结束关系但仍住在前妻娘家村庄的英国男性。受访男性45至81岁，全部比妻子年长，多数已经退休。[L05]

Between 2021 and 2022, Patcharin Lapanun and colleagues conducted multi-sited ethnographic research in Udon Thani and Khon Kaen with 32 participants: 13 Thai–Western couples, five Thai wives whose husbands were not present during the interview, and one British man whose relationship with a Thai wife had ended but who continued living in her natal village. The men were aged 45 to 81, all were older than their wives, and most were retired.[L05]

研究中的所有泰国妻子都承担某种丈夫照护，但家庭分工并非一个固定模型。有的家庭由男性提供主要收入、女性做饭清洁；也有丈夫参与家务、照顾儿童或病人，夫妻共同经营生意。女性把家务描述为协商和安排，也有人将家庭管理视为“women’s authority”。这项研究能支持的不是“泰国妻子天生会照顾人”，而是照护是一组具体劳动：做饭、清洁、购物、陪伴、照顾儿童和病人、协调亲属关系；它会受到收入、共同居住、文化适应和双方协商影响。[L05]

All the Thai wives in the study performed some form of care for their husbands, but household labour did not follow one fixed model. In some families, men provided the principal income while women cooked and cleaned. In others, husbands took part in housework or cared for children and ill relatives, while couples ran businesses together. Women described domestic work as a matter of negotiation and arrangement; some treated household management as “women’s authority.” The study does not support the claim that Thai wives are naturally caring. It shows care as a set of concrete tasks—cooking, cleaning, shopping, companionship, caring for children and sick relatives, and coordinating kin relationships—shaped by income, co-residence, cultural adaptation, and negotiation between partners.[L05]

临终阶段的证据则更少，也更沉重。Kwanchanok Jaisuekun与Sirijit Sunanta在芭堤雅进行十个月民族志；较大项目共访问41名利益相关者，但论文关于临终照护的核心叙述只来自 **3名52至57岁的泰国女性**，她们照顾的丈夫或前夫为65至82岁。[U10] 三人的经历包括全天候身体照护、陪诊与医疗协调、情绪安抚、经济压力和就业中断；她们并非作出相同选择，有人承担，有人协商，也有人拒绝。

Evidence about the end of life is both scarcer and more difficult. Kwanchanok Jaisuekun and Sirijit Sunanta conducted ten months of ethnographic research in Pattaya. Although the larger project involved 41 stakeholders, the article’s central accounts of end-of-life care came from only **three Thai women aged 52 to 57**, whose husbands or former husbands were aged 65 to 82.[U10] Their experiences included round-the-clock bodily care, hospital accompaniment and medical coordination, emotional support, financial pressure, and interrupted employment. They did not make the same choices: one accepted care responsibilities, another negotiated them, and another refused.

这三段生命史不能变成“泰国妻子普遍如何”的结论。它们只能让一个长期被抽象为“泰国照护文化”的过程显形：当丈夫无法负担私人养老或无法完整进入两国社会保障体系时，谁实际接住了他的身体？

These three life histories cannot become a general claim about “what Thai wives do.” They can only make visible a process often abstracted as “Thai caring culture”: when a husband cannot afford private eldercare or falls between the social-protection systems of two countries, who actually receives and supports his body?

新闻中的专业照护市场提供了另一面。Al Jazeera关于清迈失智照护机构的纪录片呈现，泰国女性照护者与外国老人建立亲密关系，同时为了工作离开自己的母亲和孩子。[N13] 这并不是泰西婚姻证据，却提醒我们：所谓“善于照顾人的泰国女性”不是一种无成本的文化天性。照护可能是有薪劳动，也可能在婚姻中成为无薪的 **care work（照护劳动）** 与 **emotional labour（情感劳动）**；无论哪一种，它都使用照护者的时间、身体和情绪。

The professional care market reported in the news offers another perspective. An Al Jazeera documentary about a dementia-care facility in Chiang Mai showed Thai women care workers forming intimate bonds with foreign elderly residents while leaving their own mothers and children in order to work.[N13] This is not evidence about Thai–Western marriage, but it reminds us that the supposedly natural caregiving ability of Thai women is neither costless nor simply cultural. Care may be paid labour, or it may become unpaid **care work** and **emotional labour** within marriage. Either way, it consumes a caregiver’s time, body, and emotions.

照护中的实际控制是否等于赋权？答案不能预先写好。妻子可能因为掌握医疗信息、家庭安排和丈夫日常需求而获得更大的决策空间；她也可能因为睡眠不足、工作中断和缺乏替代服务而更难离开。权力如果来自别人必须依赖自己的无偿劳动，它既可能带来影响力，也可能是一种被私人化的责任。

Does practical control within care amount to empowerment? The answer cannot be decided in advance. A wife may gain greater decision-making scope because she controls medical information, household arrangements, and her husband’s daily needs. She may also find it harder to leave because of sleep deprivation, interrupted employment, and the absence of substitute care. When power derives from another person’s dependence on one’s unpaid labour, it may create influence while also imposing a privatised responsibility.

因此，更重要的问题不是“妻子是否终于掌权”，而是：她承担照护时获得了什么保障？这份劳动是否得到承认、休息、收入或社会保险？丈夫的养老金和资产是否能转化为她在关系结束后的住房与生活安全？现有材料对前两个问题只有质性提示，对最后一个问题则几乎没有系统答案。

The more important question, then, is not whether the wife has finally taken power, but what protection she receives while providing care. Is this work met with recognition, rest, income, or social insurance? Can her husband’s pension and assets be converted into housing and livelihood security after the relationship ends? The available material offers only qualitative indications on the first questions and almost no systematic answer to the last.

## 六｜在他去世之后

### After He Dies

跨国婚姻的报道常停在两个时间点：相遇时的年龄差与金钱差，或者丈夫生病时妻子的照护。死亡之后的生活却很少被继续追踪。

Reporting on transnational marriage often stops at one of two moments: the age and income gaps visible at the couple’s first encounter, or the wife’s care when her husband becomes ill. Life after his death is rarely followed.

现有研究偶尔提到房屋登记、婚前协议、丈夫去世或前妻继续照护，但没有足够资料回答：泰国妻子能否持续获得遗属养老金？遗嘱与不同国家的继承法如何衔接？房屋或土地登记在谁名下，是否真正意味着长期居住安全？未婚伴侣拥有什么权利？如果关系在疾病前后破裂，谁能够离开，谁承担债务和照护后果？[L03][U05][U10]

Existing research occasionally mentions property registration, prenuptial agreements, a husband’s death, or a former wife continuing to provide care. It does not provide enough evidence to answer whether a Thai wife can continue receiving a survivor’s pension; how wills interact with inheritance law across countries; whether the name on a house or land title truly guarantees long-term housing security; what rights an unmarried partner possesses; or, if a relationship ends around the time of illness, who can leave and who carries the resulting debt and care burden.[L03][U05][U10]

这些不是可以用常识补齐的空白。当前2020至2026年的文献仍缺少对丧偶、继承、住房保障、离婚和关系退出的系统追踪；临终照护没有可靠的分布数据，最新全国夫妻结构研究仍依赖2010年人口普查。这里能做的不是宣布结局，而是把未知明确写出来。

These gaps cannot be filled with common sense. The literature from 2020 to 2026 still lacks systematic follow-up on widowhood, inheritance, housing security, divorce, and relationship exit. There are no reliable distributional data on end-of-life care, and the latest national study of couple composition still relies on the 2010 census. The responsible course is not to announce an ending, but to state clearly what remains unknown.

当西方丈夫在泰国老去，经济优势可能仍在，身体依赖也可能加深；泰国妻子的地方能力与家庭影响力可能上升，照护成本也可能同时增加。没有哪一种变化足以单独定义整段关系。

As Western husbands grow old in Thailand, economic advantages may persist while bodily dependence deepens. Thai wives’ local capacities and influence within the household may increase at the same time as the costs of care. No single change is sufficient to define the relationship as a whole.

真正发生的，或许是权力被拆开了：一方仍握有养老金、护照和跨国资产，另一方掌握语言、亲属网络、医疗导航和让日常生活继续运转的劳动。谁更有权力，不能脱离时间、地点和身体来回答；而谁最终得到保障，现有研究甚至还没有给出足够答案。

What may be happening is that power has been disaggregated. One partner retains a pension, passport, and transnational assets; the other controls language, kinship networks, healthcare navigation, and the labour that keeps daily life functioning. Who has more power cannot be answered apart from time, place, and the body. Existing research does not yet provide an adequate answer to the more consequential question of who ultimately receives protection.

这正是跨国退休生活最少被看见的部分。阳光、低成本和“第二人生”的许诺，可以在广告和迁移决定中被计算；但一段关系如何穿过疾病、照护、死亡与之后的生活，仍主要由家庭私下承担，也仍在新闻和研究的视野之外。

This is the least visible part of transnational retirement. Sunshine, lower costs, and the promise of a “second life” can be calculated in advertisements and migration decisions. How a relationship passes through illness, care, death, and the life that follows is still largely borne in private by families—and remains beyond much of journalism’s and research’s field of vision.
